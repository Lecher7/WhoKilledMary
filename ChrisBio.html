Chris De Garmo

  • Full name: Christopher Lee DeGarmo
  • Nicknames: Guitarmo, DeGuitarmo
  • Birthdate: June 14, 1963
  • Birthplace: Wenatchee, WA
  • Siblings: Older brother Mark (who provided the camerawork for the PL CD-ROM) and younger brother Kevin
  • Instruments: Guitar, vocals, piano, keyboards, cello, violin, sitar, etc.
  • Musical Influences: The Beatles, Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, Aerosmith, Rush, Van Halen, Iron Maiden, Judas Priest
  • Guitar heroes: Jimmy Page, David Gilmour, Eddie Van Halen
    Some current favorite artists: Peter Gabriel, Alice in Chains, Stone Temple Pilots, Tool, Tin Machine/David Bowie, Soundgarden, Tori Amos
  • Marital Status: Married to Kimberly DeGarmo, with two children, Rylie and Preston
  • Hobbies: Flying, golf, reading, etc.

 


Some tidbits...

* Mr. DeGarmo is best known as the former guitarist of Queensryche, the Seattle/Bellevue-based band who formed in 1982. As one of the founding members of the band, Chris named the band, taken from the "Queen of the Reich" song on their first release, the Queensryche EP. He left the band in mid-November of 1997. "Creative differences" was the official reason. On Jan. 30 '98, he sent this statement to Queensryche website Screaming in Digital: "I thought I might clear up a misconception or two. I did decide to end my participation in Queensrÿche in mid-November '97 (the South America shows were previously committed to). My decision had nothing to do with Peter Cornell. I am very proud of everything we (QR) have created and achieved together. I wish each member individually, and collectively, the very best. I love them, they are like brothers." Other statements by DeGarmo-- he told Kerrang magazine in June '98 that it was time to move on.

* After Queensryche broke it off with their first managers, Harris Management, in 1986-87, Chris managed the band for several months, grabbing a spot on the Bon Jovi European tour for the band and some select headlining dates on the West Coast and New York.

* Chris wrote majority of the band's biggest commercial hits, including "Silent Lucidity," an BMI Award-winning song, "Sign of the Times," "Jet City Woman," "Eyes of a Stranger"-- the track that broke Queensryche into the AOR charts, "Queen of the Reich," etc., not to mention most of the fan-faves like "Take Hold of the Flame," "Suite Sister Mary," "The Whisper," "Neue Regel," and so on.

* "Silent Lucidity" went on to win Grammy nominations and won the 1991 Billboard Rock Song of the Year and its video, directed by Matt Mahurin, won the 1991 MTV Video Music "Viewer's Choice" Award. The video was also #4 on MTV's 1991 top videos of the year list.

* Queensryche album sales: US RIAA figures-- Empire 1990 (triple platinum), Operation: Livecrime 1991 (double platinum), Promised Land 1994 (platinum), Operation: Mindcrime 1988 (platinum), Rage For Order 1986 (gold), The Warning 1984 (gold), Queensryche EP 1983 (gold); Video sales-- Building Empire 1992 (gold), Video: Mindcrime 1989 (platinum)

* Outside projects: Chris worked with Michael Kamen, played a few shows with Peter Cornell, currently of the band Grace, and was the second guitarist for Jerry Cantrell for his Boggy Depot tour. Chris' work with Kamen are featured on two official releases, the Last Action Hero Original Soundtrack, which he reportedly does some slide and rhythm work on a few tracks, and Michael Kamen's Opus album.

* CD showed his multi-instrumental talent on Promised Land by playing sitar, cello, violin/viola and piano on various tracks such as "I Am I," "Lady Jane" and "Someone Else?"

* Chris debuted his lead vocals on "All I Want," a track on the Hear in the Now Frontier album. While it was a surprise for most Queensryche fans since Geoff Tate, Queensryche's vocalist, is very known for his vocal ability, Tate said in interviews that DeGarmo usually comes up with the vocal melodies for the songs (lyrics) he has written and it was only natural for DeGarmo to sing it considering how personal lyric was.

* More vocal work shown on the Jerry Cantrell Boggy Depot tour. Since Alice in Chains and Cantrell songs featured much backing vocals and some co-lead work, DeGarmo did quite a lot of singing. He sang on every song except "Cut You In" which he opted to concentrate on the slide instead.
