Queensryche
  

It was back in 1980 that Mike Wilton happened upon Scott Rockenfield in a record store
called Easy Street Records.  Both were big fans of a new band from England called Iron
Maiden, and soon they had hooked up to form Cross Fire, a band that would also include
a young Chris Degarmo.  Their original bass player, Dave Hovland, was later replaced
by a high school friend of Scott's called Eddie Jackson.  At this point, Cross Fire
became The Mob, taking their name from the Ronnie James Dio-era Black Sabbath album.

The Mob were booked to do a multi-band rock festival called Metalfest '81, but found
themselves without a singer.  They had heard of a guy named Jeff Tate who fronted
the bands Babylon and Myth and liked what they heard.  Mustering up the courage, the
members of The Mob called Jeff and asked if he could do the show with them.  Babylon
was already on the bill, so Jeff thought ti would be fun to play with 2 different
bands on the same night.

The crowd reaction was much better than anticipated, and all involved were quite
impressed with the chemistry between them.  Geoff played a few more parties with The
Mob, but his heart wasn't into the Metal Coverband scene; he was quickly becoming a
fan of Peter Gabriel and longed for a more visually artistic approach to music.

The Mob asked Jeff for one more favor; they were cutting a demo and wanted him to sing
on it.  Jeff agreed, much to the dismay of the bandmembers of Myth.  Geoff wanted the
experience of working in a professional studio, and convinced the members of Myth that
it would benefit them in the long run.

And so it came to pass that Jeff Tate would end up in Triad Studios with the members of
The Mob to record the demo that would eventually become Queensryche's first release.
That session yielded 4 songs; Queen Of The Reich, Nightrider, Blinded, and a song Chris
had written the music for but didn't have words called The Lady Wore Black.  Jeff wrote
the words that week while they were in the studio.

It should be noted that both Kelly Gray and Randy "Random Damage" Gane were in and
around the studio when the EP was being recorded.  Jeff would not become an official
member until a full year later.

As the story goes, the members of Myth had just had one fight too many, and Jeff and
Kelly both became upset.  A very short time afterwards, the editors of KERRANG!
magazine had received a copy of the Mob demos and had written a review proclaiming
them to be the future of Heavy Metal!  Things were moving fast, mail was piling up,
and the EP was released independantly on the "206" label.  The release exploded
worldwide, and Jeff had no choice but to become a permanent member.  The name
Queensryche was chosen as their new name, taken directly from the song title from the
opening track of the EP.

Their first official gig was opening up for Zebra on June 29th, 1983 at the Portland
Paramount Theater.  They went on to tour with Quiet Riot, Twisted Sister and Dio on
that first tour, ending in a headlining gig at The Ritz in New York City on
November 1st, 1983.
