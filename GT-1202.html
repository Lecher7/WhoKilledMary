Talking with . . .   Geoff Tate

Interviewed 12/16/2002 by Brett VanPut

Interview with vocalist Geoff Tate
Vocalist Geoff Tate is best known for his legandary, genre defining work with Queensryche. Despite rumors of internal strife and an evolution of their trademark sound, Tate's career has been declining just as he prepares to embark on a solo career. His debut self titled disc is a lot different than fans expected as it is much more mature and rock oriented. I caught Tate's show in New Jersey this summer and was pleasantly surprised to see him stay after the show for hours to chat and sign for an enthusiastic crowd. This was after a nearly two hour performance. Here is a conversation I had with Geoff shortly after over the phone.

Where are you at now?
We're in Tuscon, Arizona.

How's the tour been so far?
Excellent, I'm having a great time.

What do you think of your critics who want your solo material to sound like Queensryche?
Music is all about evolving anyway. You can't just do the same thing everytime. It becomes static. Writing music and making records is such an absorbed exercise. It's all about pleasing yourself. Then you share this with the world and there are going to be people who like it and people who don't like it. If you always try to please everyone, you will end up not pleasing anyone. I've always gone into it with the idea of pleasing myself and making something that I feel good about.

Any chance of you working with Chris DeGarmo again in the future?
I suppose there's a slim chance. I wouldn't say no, but I don't look forward to that happening in the near future.

Are there any musicians you would like to work with?
I would like to work with some people who have a jazz background. I can't think of specific people offhand. The people who I am touring with are people I pursued. I saw them playing with different people around town and I was familiar with the records they had worked on. After meeting them I felt there was a connection there so I went after them to collaborate with.

What approach do you take with your solo material as opposed to your approach with Queensryche?
This one was so much more open ended. We had people who came into it from a non rock background, they were throwing out ideas that were really strange and new to me. That took a little while for me to grasp and accept the possibilities. Right now working with Queensryche it's up to me to do the same sort of thing and convince them into changing their writing approach. For this album I had so much material to choose from, we had to whittle it down. I wanted to do a record that was a reflection of my musical influences. A lot of it I haven't been able to explore with Queensryche. I am influenced by r &b and soul, I really like a lot of electronic music that's out now, jazz. I'm very excited about incorporating more jazz into my solo career.

Do you think people can get an accurate glimpse into you as a person through your music?
I think you would have to look through all the lyrics and the musical experimentation over the years and put together a rounded picture but even then it's just a partial thing.

How much is music a part of your life?
It's been consuming quite a bit. I tend to be one of those real extreme personalities, I throw myself into projects or whatever I'm doing, fully. I've been trying over the past few years to balance myself and not dive in over my head all of the time. I have a family and I like to balance my time a little.

Does your family go out on the road with you?
Sometimes they do, yeah.

How do social events influence your writing?
Social circumstances, social issues, definitely effect a writer. In some ways, like Mindcrime, we try to make something out of it. Other times it can be a commentary, like on this record there's a song called "Off the T.V." which is a commentary about being obsessed with people watching other people's lives.

How do you feel about being the vocalist who has influenced an entire genre of music?
One thing that they don't get is that it's really about songwriting. There's some really talented solos, fast players, and performers with great dexterity, but it's all about the song and they couldn't write their way out of a paper bag. I'm most interested in the song. I'm not interested in guitar gymnastics or vocal gymnastics. I've done all that already. I wrote a song like "Before the Storm" off of The Warning which is a complete exercise in showing off. I don't need to do that anymore. I'm into a completely different thing. I don't see it as a competition. I don't mean that in an elitist sense, it's simply where I'm at now.

Are relationships an important aspect of your writing?
I think relationships are fascinating and the most important subject matter. Without relationships you can't grow, emotionally all your areas are stifled. There's all kinds of interesting angles to look at. The main idea of this record was to explore relationships. I tried to do that on a number of different levels.

Is there any chance of Operation Mindcrime becoming a film?
Over the years I've met with a number of different people about it. Not until lately, have I met someone who saw it in the right kind of spirit. I find a guy based out of L.A. who is a writer and producer for a company. So far that looks like it might pan out. We're in the screenwriting period right now. You get to a certain point and sometimes it works out and other times it doesn't, but so far this is looking pretty good.

Will you have input?
The way movies work, you never know. I would like to keep as much creative control as possible. I've written a screenplay for it, whether they follow that screenplay or not is up in the air right now. My goal is to have as much creative control as possible.

Why did you choose to open your solo show with two old Queensryche songs and rearrange them?
I've always wanted to change the Queensryche songs a little and I tried to open up the guys to experimenting with the arrangements.

What is your mindframe entering this new Queensryche disc?
I should explain this to you. The way the Queensryche record is put together is I wrote and co-wrote a bunch of songs with different people, I then took these songs in to Michael, Scott, and Eddie for the Queensryche record. I asked them to record these for me and make the changes they need to make it sound the way they want them to, then I will come in and add my stuff on top. They're recording the songs right now that I've written with other people. I haven't heard what they've done to them yet. When I go into the studio in August, that will be the first time I hear what they've done to them. They aren't going to change drastically but they will be changed somewhat.

Will you have a second guitarist in the studio?
No we'll just have Michael in the studio. I hope to just have Michael on the tour as well.

Any final comments?
You may have heard I had my saxophone stolen in New Jersey. I got it back. I think the people who stole it started to feel guilty about it. They sent it back to me. I got it in Austin, Texas about four or five days ago.
